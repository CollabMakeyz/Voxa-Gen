0048|Distortion
0049|Hard, gritty clipping for aggressive or broken voices.
0050|Distortion drives the voice into a clipper. New harmonics pile up, and loud parts get dirtier than quiet ones: angry, broken, a blown-out speaker. Villains and screams live here. Drive is how hard it hits, Type the kind of clipping, Tone takes the edge off the top. Start at 12-18 dB of drive with 30-50 % mix.
0051|Saturation
0052|Gentle tape or tube warmth; thicker without sounding broken.
0053|Saturation rounds off the peaks and adds a few low harmonics. Fuller, warmer, still clean. That's what sets it apart from Distortion. The level is matched to the input, so it won't just get louder. Good for a thin sample or an old-record feel. Character picks tape (smooth top) or tube (even harmonics); Warmth adds asymmetry. 6-10 dB of drive stays subtle.
0054|Formant Shift
0055|Changes the voice's timbre (size of the throat) without changing the note.
0056|Formant Shift moves the resonances of the vocal tract and leaves the note alone. Positive values sound smaller and younger (cartoon, chipmunk); negative ones bigger and deeper (giant, monster). The chromatic stays in tune. It runs a voice analysis, so it's slower than the other effects. ±2-4 semitones still sounds natural.
0057|Tremolo
0058|Pulses the VOLUME up and down at a steady speed.
0059|Tremolo moves the volume up and down; the pitch stays put. The voice pulses, stutters or wobbles. Shaky, nervous, retro, or square waves for a chopped glitch. Rate is pulses per second, Depth how deep the dips go, Shape the curve of each pulse. 5-7 Hz at 50 % depth is the classic wobble.
0060|Vibrato
0061|Wobbles the PITCH up and down around the note at a steady speed.
0062|Vibrato raises and lowers the pitch at a steady speed, the way a singer does. The average stays on the note. Expressive held notes, old cartoons, or seasick at high depth. Rate is wobbles per second, Depth how far the pitch moves in cents (100 cents = 1 semitone), Onset how long it waits to fade in. 5-6 Hz and 20-40 cents sounds natural.
0063|Pitch Variation
0064|Random, slow pitch drift around the note (unsteady, alive).
0065|Pitch Variation lets the pitch wander around the note, randomly but smoothly, with the average still in tune. There's no steady rhythm like Vibrato has, so it comes out unsteady, drunk, ghostly, or just more human. The randomness is fixed per note: preview and export match. Depth is the largest deviation in cents, Speed how often it turns. Under 20 cents stays subtle.
0066|BitCrusher
0067|Lo-fi digital grit: fewer bits and a lower sample rate.
0068|BitCrusher lowers the bit depth and the sample rate of the voice. Fewer bits bring hiss and harsh steps; a lower rate brings metallic aliasing. 8-bit, old consoles, broken radio. 8 bits at about 11 kHz is the classic console; 4 bits is wrecked.
0069|Double Pitch
0070|Adds a transposed copy of the voice (octave, fifth…) on top.
0071|Double Pitch adds a second voice a musical interval away, like singing in octaves or harmony. The original stays as it is; the copy comes in at the level you set. -12 gives a monster layer, +12 a cartoon one, +7 a power-chord feel. Detune makes the two voices beat against each other. Formants decides whether the copy keeps the original timbre or follows the pitch. It uses voice analysis, so it's slower.
0072|Frequency Shifter
0073|Moves every frequency by the same number of Hz (metallic, inharmonic).
0074|The Frequency Shifter adds the same number of hertz to every part of the sound. A pitch shift keeps the harmonics in proportion; this breaks them. The voice goes metallic, alien or bell-like, and a little out of tune. 5-30 Hz gives a phasing shimmer; 100 Hz and up sounds robotic. Positive goes up, negative goes down.
0075|Chorus
0076|Several slightly detuned, delayed copies: a wider, ensemble voice.
0077|Chorus mixes in copies of the voice on short, slowly moving delays. Tiny pitch and timing differences make it sound like several singers, wider and softer. Dreamy, choir-like, or a ghost. Voices is how many copies, Rate and Depth how much they move, Delay how far behind they sit, Spread how wide they go in stereo.
0078|Phaser
0079|Sweeping notches that make the voice swirl like a jet.
0080|The Phaser mixes the voice with a phase-shifted copy, which cuts moving notches into the spectrum. It swooshes and swirls: underwater, spacey, psychedelic. Rate is the sweep speed, Depth how far it sweeps, Center where the notches sit, Stages how many there are, Feedback how sharply they ring.
0081|Ring Modulator
0082|Multiplies the voice by a tone: robotic, bell-like, Dalek voices.
0083|The Ring Modulator multiplies the voice by a second tone, the carrier. The rhythm of the words survives, but the pitch turns into metallic sum and difference tones. That's the old robot voice. With the carrier following the note, the robot sounds the same across the whole chromatic; a fixed carrier gives each note its own colour. 30-60 % mix keeps the words understandable.
0084|Noise
0085|Adds hiss, rumble or vinyl crackle under the voice.
0086|Noise puts a noise layer under the voice. Its level is relative to the voice: quiet and loud samples get the same amount. Follow makes it rise and fall with the voice, and the gaps between notes stay clean. Radio, old tape, whispers, breathy horror. Types: white (bright hiss), pink (soft hiss), brown (rumble), crackle (vinyl).
0087|Shapes the tone: cut rumble, add presence, make it darker or brighter.
0088|The EQ boosts or cuts frequency ranges. Low cut clears rumble and mic noise. The low shelf adds or takes away body, the mid band handles presence and nasal tone, the high shelf adds air or darkness, and high cut removes hiss or fakes a phone or radio. Moves of 2-4 dB are usually plenty. Everything at 0 dB with the cuts at their limits changes nothing.
